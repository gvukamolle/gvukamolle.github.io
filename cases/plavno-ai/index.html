The challenge

Plavno evolved from an OpenWebUI fork, but the product goal was broader than a corporate chat. It had to become a unified workspace for the company’s AI capabilities: a shared platform and knowledge layer with specialized products embedded into real operations.

I owned Plavno end to end as its internal customer, analyst, product owner, and delivery owner. I ran discovery, researched interfaces, directed AI agents, personally tested user journeys, and made the final production-rollout decisions.

At the architecture level, I defined component boundaries, integrations, data flows, contracts, and risks. AI agents implemented the code; infrastructure specialists supported hosting, and one specialist supported model deployment.

Product architecture

The platform brings together five connected product areas:

  1. Plavno core and Sol — chat, corporate knowledge, RAG, tools, skills, and governed invocation contracts.
  2. Studio — documents, notes, smart tables, and Canvas boards that are automatically available to the knowledge layer; Sol can work directly in the context of the open page.
  3. Calendar, Mail, and Speech — everyday communications, meeting capture, transcription, and follow-up across web, iOS, and Android.
  4. B2POS — log analysis that helps business users and L2 support investigate problematic credit applications faster.
  5. Contact Center — analysis of employee work across the complete credit application rather than a single call.

A shared shell and access model let these areas develop as one product system instead of a collection of disconnected AI demos.

Key product decisions

In B2POS, the analysis works across four log sources, with all four routes in production. It supports roughly 40 investigations per day. Before the workflow, an investigation could take from 30 minutes to two days; specialists now usually complete one in about ten minutes. This is an operating observation, not automated time tracking.

The contact-center request began as call scoring. Discovery with the department lead showed that judging an employee without the complete application context would be misleading. The unit of analysis therefore became the application: roughly 40 applications and 90 calls per day within the limited conversion-stage workflow. The product supports more grounded bonus allocation and coaching; I do not claim a time saving.

Sol runs as Plavno’s own harness with dedicated skills, tools, and invocation contracts. I considered moving Sol to an experimental deep-agent architecture, but kept the harness inside the platform to preserve control over its behavior and product integrations. Deep Agent is used separately for log analysis. I also researched familiar interface patterns and rejected requests that did not create clear user, business, or system value.

Delivery approach

The cycle combined business discovery, review of the existing process and dependencies, a test-environment MVP, demonstration, solution refinement, agent implementation, my own testing of journeys and data, user acceptance, and an explicit rollout decision. I also delivered two presentations and training sessions for approximately 100 business and IT participants.

Outcome

Plavno’s built-in product analytics reported approximately 50 DAU, 120 WAU, and 200 MAU across July and August 2026. The figures are available through an internal analytics MCP server; most detailed metrics remain confidential.

The platform is used by testers, analysts, business teams, the contact center, and operations. Speech remains a substantial product area across web and mobile. Production data, B2POS, contact-center interfaces, and organizational structure are protected; only anonymized screens, diagrams, and approved modules can be shown publicly.
