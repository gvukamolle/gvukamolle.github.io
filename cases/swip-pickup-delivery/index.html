Context and constraint

Leadership wanted to build a competitive pickup-and-delivery offer using the company’s existing partner base. The company had neither a ready delivery network nor a unified operating flow.

Menu data was the critical integration constraint. The existing 1C setup could not by itself support the required customer and partner flow, while store-level availability and product add-ons still had to remain accurate.

Product decision

I framed pickup and delivery as one connected product across the consumer application and an administrative interface. The existing 1C menu integration could not support the full journey, so we built an admin import tool connected to cash-register systems for availability and add-on checks.

This gave the business an interface it could manage while preserving the store-level availability information shown to customers.

What I owned

I owned discovery, customer journeys, requirements, prioritization, coordination of the six-person team, testing, acceptance, and launch. This was a consumer-product case without AI.

Delivery system

The work combined product discovery with partner-network operations. Consumer and admin journeys were designed together, integration constraints were resolved before acceptance, and the launch was evaluated as a complete operating flow rather than an isolated application feature.

Outcome

Retrospective estimate for my tenure: the service handled approximately 300 orders per day across 60 locations in eight cities, from Moscow to Vladivostok. After the June 2025 launch, I handed the product over to support. I left in November when the work had fully shifted from product development to maintenance.
